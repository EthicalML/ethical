My talk from PyCon DE & PyData 2026 is now available to [watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I1GvlW1H4WI). I used it to look at production ML across a twenty-year window, from the early MLOps days of 2015 through to where the discipline may land by 2035, which is quite a lot of ground for one session.

The message I keep coming back to is that the lifecycle of a machine learning model only begins once it is in production. The first half traces the 2015-2023 evolution and the shift of engineering effort from training towards inference and the application layer, together with the operational artifacts that arrived with it, namely prompts and vector databases.

The second half covers monitoring, stack alignment, shorter production timelines and more autonomous operational patterns. I close on managing platform complexity and reducing operational debt. Do let me know what you think.
