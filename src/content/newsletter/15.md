## [AI debiasing doesn't debias bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03862)

There has been a lot of very interesting research in the area of algorithmic bias. [This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03862) is no exception - a great insight on how some of the debiasing methods propsed in previous research only cover up the systematic gender biases identified as opposed to actually removing them. Very recommended (relatively short) read: ["Lipstick on a Pig: Debiasing Methods Cover up Systematic Gender Biases in Word Embeddings But do not Remove Them"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03862).

## [The NLP of human noises](https://s3-us-west-1.amazonaws.com/vocs/map.html?mkt_tok=eyJpIjoiWWpSaVpETTJNMlV4T0RRNSIsInQiOiJ2eEVHalByUTZaZXlGRHBNeVJlUU8xcjcrYk5mTXZlVFZHMHprNFZyRDFGNWlJbmNnNXhKbGc4Mk4wV3doWXRnaWFYZ1RwdlRHZHFVSVlIK0tPR3VrU3lnYUdEcHlUNFFoSG5kSDNYcUp1aEhtdVgrTGdhVjJtNnRlOG11b1VSRiJ9#)

Very interesting [piece of research](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329824563_Mapping_24_Emotions_Conveyed_by_Brief_Human_Vocalization) that goes beyond words. This paper dives into the noises the humans vocalise during conversations. These include all the "hmm"s, "umm"s, "ehem"s and beyond. What's better is that they actually made available a really interesting interactive visualisation which allows us to see how [these sounds are clustered together](https://distill.pub/2019/activation-atlas/). Try it out by hovering with your mouse (you may want to use headphones or lower the volume of your laptop).

## [Feature visualisation via activation](https://distill.pub/2019/activation-atlas/)

This [interesting post](https://distill.pub/2019/activation-atlas/) goes beyond feature importance and introduces the concept of "feature visualisation". As they put it, feature visualisation allows us to wear "lenses" to look through the eyes of the network. This is great as it promises to help us answer the question of "What have these networks learned that allows them to classify images so well?". This area could complement metrics such as feature importance and introduce explainability into models that could then allow domain experts to interpret the reasoning behind complex networks.

## [The GAN stroke of genious](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/2019/03/18/gaugan-photorealistic-landscapes-nvidia-research/)

NVIDIA comes back with a very [interesting piece of research](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/2019/03/18/gaugan-photorealistic-landscapes-nvidia-research/). They use a deep neural network to convert doodles into photorealistic images. This could be a great tool for graphic designers (and other creative jobs), allowing experts to prototype and experiment faster before diving into their craft. In [their video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p5U4NgVGAwg) they actually show how this tool can be used. We may finally be able to keep up with the [legendary tutorials of Bob Ross](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B5Wo1ubuzAE) thanks to these type of tools.

## MLOps = [Featured OS Libraries](https://github.com/EthicalML/awesome-machine-learning-operations#12-function-as-a-service-frameworks)

We are excited to see the [Awesome MLOps](https://github.com/EthicalML/awesome-machine-learning-operations) list growing to over 300 stars now! Thanks to everyone for your support! This week's edition is focused on new libraries on Privacy Preserving Machine Learning which fall on our Responsible ML Principle [#6](http://ethical.institute/principles.html#commitment-7). The four featured libraries this week are:

- [Tensorflow Privacy](https://github.com/tensorflow/privacy) - A Python library that includes implementations of TensorFlow optimizers for training machine learning models with differential privacy.
- [TF-Encrypted](https://github.com/mortendahl/tf-encrypted) - A Python library built on top of TensorFlow for researchers and practitioners to experiment with privacy-preserving machine learning.
- [PySyft](https://github.com/OpenMined/PySyft) - A Python library for secure, private Deep Learning. PySyft decouples private data from model training, using Multi-Party Computation (MPC) within PyTorch.
- [Uber SQL Differencial Privacy](https://github.com/uber/sql-differential-privacy) - Uber's open source framework that enforces differential privacy for general-purpose SQL queries.

If you know of any libraries that are not in the ["Awesome MLOps"](https://github.com/EthicalML/awesome-machine-learning-operations) list, please do give us a heads up or feel free to [add a pull request](https://github.com/EthicalML/awesome-machine-learning-operations)!
