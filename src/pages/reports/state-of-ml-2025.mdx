findings:
  primary-ml-library:
    headline: PyTorch leads, but the library field remains split.
    insight: PyTorch, Lightning and Fast.ai account for 35% of answers. Scikit-learn follows at 33%, two points higher than in 2024.
  modalities:
    headline: LLM work and time series both moved sharply upward.
    insight: LLMs reached 59%, up 12 points year on year. Time series rose even faster to 58%, a 16-point gain.
  use-cases:
    headline: Demand forecasting became the dominant reported use case.
    insight: It was selected by 52% of respondents, up 20 points from 2024. Recommenders follow at 38%, close to last year's share.
  time-to-production:
    headline: Production cycles shifted toward the three-month mark.
    insight: Forty-one percent report taking one to three months, ten points more than in 2024. The three-to-six-month group fell by 12 points.
  cloud-platform:
    headline: AWS still leads, while Azure and GCP gained ground.
    insight: AWS remains first at 32%, but its share is 22 points below 2024. Azure also has 32%, while GCP reaches 26%.
  production-challenges:
    headline: Monitoring is the most common production challenge.
    insight: Forty-two percent selected monitoring model performance. Access to training data and robust data pipelines follow at 37% and 34%.
  experiment-tracking:
    headline: MLflow has consolidated its lead in experiment tracking.
    insight: Two thirds of respondents who use a tracking or registry tool chose MLflow. Its share is 18 points higher than in 2024.
  feature-store:
    headline: Most feature-store users still rely on in-house systems.
    insight: In-house tooling leads at 55%, though seven points below 2024. FEAST doubled its share to 20%.
  vector-database:
    headline: Vector database choices remain notably fragmented.
    insight: Pinecone leads at 21%, followed by in-house systems at 18%. Both gained roughly seven to eight points year on year.
  workflow-orchestrator:
    headline: Airflow remains the default orchestrator.
    insight: Airflow leads at 47%, six points above 2024. Databricks is a distant second at 16%, but gained 12 points.
  training-platform:
    headline: Databricks moved to the front of training platforms.
    insight: Databricks leads at 28%, with SageMaker and Azure ML Studio both at 16%. Azure ML Studio doubled its 2024 share.
  real-time-serving:
    headline: Serving choices spread beyond Python web frameworks.
    insight: FastAPI and Flask still lead at 38%, but fell ten points. Databricks rose to 18%, a gain of 12 points.
  model-monitoring:
    headline: In-house monitoring leads, but packaged tools are gaining.
    insight: In-house systems account for 41%, 11 points below 2024. Evidently rose eight points to 27%.
  central-data-platform:
    headline: The central data platform market has no runaway leader.
    insight: Delta Lake leads at 20%, closely followed by Azure Data Lake at 19% and Snowflake at 17%. Azure gained 11 points.
  managed-foundation-model:
    headline: OpenAI leads a rapidly diversifying service market.
    insight: OpenAI accounts for 30%, down ten points. Azure AI follows at 22%, while Gemini tripled its share to 19%.
  industry:
    headline: Technology and finance anchor the respondent base.
    insight: Technology represents 21% and financial services 15%. The overall industry mix remains broad, with no single sector near a majority.
  organisation-size:
    headline: Respondents span both enterprises and small teams.
    insight: Organisations with 5,000 to 50,000 employees are the largest group at 22%. Those with 1,000 to 5,000 follow at 21%.
  models-in-production:
    headline: Most fleets are measured in tens, not thousands.
    insight: Two to five models and ten to twenty models are the two largest groups, each near 24%. Only 8% report more than 1,000.
  models-planned:
    headline: Teams expect their production fleets to keep expanding.
    insight: The largest plan is ten to twenty models at 29%, up 11 points. Plans for two to five models also rose nine points.
  organisation-setup:
    headline: Governance is becoming an operating function.
    insight: AI risk and governance teams reached 41%, up 21 points. Central ML platforms remain most common at 66%.
  real-time-inference:
    headline: Batch remains the dominant inference mode.
    insight: Forty-three percent say fewer than one in ten models run real-time inference, nine points more than in 2024.
  deployment-methods:
    headline: CI/CD is now close to universal among respondents.
    insight: Eighty-four percent report CI/CD support, up 15 points. Separate development, staging and production environments reached 66%.
  job-family:
    headline: ML engineers remain the largest practitioner group.
    insight: ML engineers account for 42% of answers. MLOps engineers follow at 22%, with data scientists close behind at 21%.
  role-level:
    headline: Individual contributors form the centre of the sample.
    insight: Junior to senior individual contributors make up 43%, five points above 2024. Staff-level and above represent 23%.
  age:
    headline: The age profile broadened beyond the early thirties.
    insight: Ages 35 to 39 are the largest group at 24%. Ages 30 to 34 fell by 13 points, while ages 40 to 44 gained seven.
  country:
    headline: Responses reflect a widely distributed community.
    insight: Germany and India are the largest consistently labelled groups at 11% each. Free-text country naming means small variants remain visible separately.
  self-identification:
    headline: The gender imbalance remains pronounced.
    insight: Ninety percent self-identify as male and 9% as female. The female share is two points higher than in 2024.
methodology:
  deck: This edition compares the 2025 answers with the same questions and answer labels from 2024. Question-level response bases are summarised here so every percentage can be read in context.
  collection: Responses were collected through the State of Production ML survey. The report aggregates the supplied CSV at build time and publishes no raw-response table.
  denominator: Each percentage uses the number of nonblank respondents to that question, so N varies. For multiple-choice questions, a bar is the share of respondents selecting that answer and totals can exceed 100%.
