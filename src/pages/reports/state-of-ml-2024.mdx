findings:
  primary-ml-library:
    headline: PyTorch led a divided library field.
    insight: PyTorch, Lightning and Fast.ai accounted for 36% of answers. Scikit-learn followed at 31%.
  modalities:
    headline: LLMs became the leading reported modality.
    insight: LLM work reached 47%, with time series at 42% and tabular machine learning at 39%.
  use-cases:
    headline: Recommenders led a broad use-case mix.
    insight: Recommender systems were selected by 39% of respondents. Demand forecasting followed at 32%.
  time-to-production:
    headline: Most production cycles stayed below six months.
    insight: Thirty-one percent reported one to three months, while 26% took three to six months and 24% less than a month.
  cloud-platform:
    headline: AWS held a clear cloud lead.
    insight: Amazon Web Services accounted for 54% of answers. Azure followed at 21% and Google Cloud Platform at 16%.
  production-challenges:
    headline: Monitoring was the defining production challenge.
    insight: Monitoring and observability was selected by 45%. Tooling gaps and access to training data followed at roughly one third.
  experiment-tracking:
    headline: MLflow set the pace for experiment tracking.
    insight: MLflow represented 48% of respondents using a tracking or registry tool. In-house systems followed at 16%.
  feature-store:
    headline: Feature stores were predominantly built in-house.
    insight: In-house systems accounted for 62% of feature-store answers. FEAST was the leading packaged option at 10%.
  vector-database:
    headline: Vector database choices remained fragmented.
    insight: Pinecone led at 14%, narrowly ahead of Milvus at 12%. No option held a dominant share.
  workflow-orchestrator:
    headline: Airflow was the default workflow orchestrator.
    insight: Airflow accounted for 41% of answers. In-house systems followed at 17% and Argo Workflows at 11%.
  training-platform:
    headline: In-house platforms led model training.
    insight: In-house systems represented 34%, followed by Databricks at 23% and Amazon SageMaker at 17%.
  real-time-serving:
    headline: Python web frameworks dominated real-time serving.
    insight: FastAPI and Flask wrappers accounted for 47%. In-house systems followed at 16% and SageMaker at 12%.
  model-monitoring:
    headline: Most monitoring stacks were built internally.
    insight: In-house systems represented 52% of answers. Evidently was the leading packaged tool at 19%.
  central-data-platform:
    headline: Data platforms had no runaway winner.
    insight: Delta Lake led at 22%, followed by AWS Lake Formation at 18% and Snowflake at 17%.
  managed-foundation-model:
    headline: OpenAI led managed foundation model services.
    insight: OpenAI accounted for 40% of answers. Azure AI followed at 21% and Amazon Bedrock at 13%.
  industry:
    headline: Technology and finance anchored the sample.
    insight: Technology represented 21% of respondents and financial services 17%, with the remainder spread across many sectors.
  organisation-size:
    headline: The sample spanned organisations of every scale.
    insight: Organisations with 50 to 250 employees were the largest group at 20%, followed by 250 to 1,000 at 18%.
  models-in-production:
    headline: Production fleets were usually measured in tens.
    insight: Two to five models was the largest group at 24%. Ten to twenty and twenty-one to one hundred followed at 20% and 19%.
  models-planned:
    headline: Planned fleets pointed toward continued expansion.
    insight: Twenty-five percent planned for 21 to 100 models. Five to nine followed at 20%, with ten to twenty at 18%.
  organisation-setup:
    headline: Central ML and data platforms were the operating core.
    insight: Central ML teams and data platform organisations were each reported by about 65% of respondents.
  real-time-inference:
    headline: Batch remained central to inference workloads.
    insight: Thirty-five percent said fewer than one in ten models ran real-time inference. Twenty-two percent reported more than nine in ten.
  deployment-methods:
    headline: CI/CD underpinned most deployment workflows.
    insight: Sixty-nine percent reported CI/CD support, while 58% had separate development, staging and production environments.
  job-family:
    headline: ML engineers formed the largest practitioner group.
    insight: Machine learning engineers accounted for 44%, followed by data scientists at 23% and MLOps engineers at 19%.
  role-level:
    headline: Individual contributors dominated the sample.
    insight: Junior to senior individual contributors represented 38%, with staff-level contributors at 31% and managers at 18%.
  age:
    headline: Respondents clustered in their early thirties.
    insight: Ages 30 to 34 represented 35% of answers. Ages 35 to 39 followed at 22%.
  country:
    headline: Responses came from a distributed international community.
    insight: The USA was the largest consistently labelled group at 18%, followed by the Netherlands at 9% and the United Kingdom at 8%.
  self-identification:
    headline: The gender imbalance was pronounced.
    insight: Ninety-three percent self-identified as male and 7% as female, with one non-binary response.
methodology:
  deck: This baseline edition reports the 2024 answers without a prior-year comparison. Question-level response bases are summarised here so every percentage can be read in context.
  collection: Responses were collected through the State of Production ML survey. The report aggregates the supplied CSV at build time and publishes no raw-response table.
  denominator: Each percentage uses the number of nonblank respondents to that question, so N varies. For multiple-choice questions, a bar is the share of respondents selecting that answer and totals can exceed 100%.
