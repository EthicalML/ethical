## United Nations

The Institute participates in United Nations AI and data-governance work at the national-scale end of the principles. Its UN CSTD committee role has a [public record](https://unctad.org/topic/commission-on-science-and-technology-for-development/working-group-on-data-governance).

## Standards bodies

ISO/IEC and IEEE AI standards work provides formal, cross-industry baselines for assessing and governing AI systems. The [AI-RFX framework](/frameworks/ai-rfx/) and [ML Maturity Model](/frameworks/maturity-model/) operationalise these baselines in procurement and development processes; they do not replace them.

ACM's Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct sets broad expectations for computing professionals. The nine Responsible AI Principles provide AI-specific commitments that practitioners can apply alongside that baseline. The Institute's ACM participation has a [public volunteer record](https://www.acm.org/volunteers/volunteer/saucedo_9722274).

## Open-source foundations & security

The Institute's collaboration with Linux Foundation AI & Data includes open-source and ML-security work. [Kompute](/open-source/kompute/) provides cross-vendor GPU acceleration, while the ML Security Committee connected this work with OpenSSF collaboration. The [joining announcement](https://lfaidata.foundation/blog/2019/10/09/the-institute-for-ethical-ai-and-machine-learning-joins-lf-ai/) provides the public record.

NumFOCUS publishes a [case study of the Institute's contribution](https://numfocus.org/case-studies/ethics-in-ai-ml). The Institute was also an official reviewer of the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications, which connects to the Institute's [agentic and ML security framework](/frameworks/security/) and the [OWASP announcement](https://genai.owasp.org/2025/12/09/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-the-benchmark-for-agentic-security-in-the-age-of-autonomous-ai/).
