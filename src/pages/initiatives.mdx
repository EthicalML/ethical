## Standards contributions

The Institute participates in ISO/IEC AI standards work, which sets the formal, cross-industry baseline for how AI systems are assessed and governed. Its practical frameworks — AI-RFX and the ML Maturity Model — are built to be compatible with that baseline rather than to replace it: where ISO/IEC defines the standard, the frameworks are one way to operationalise it inside an organisation's own procurement and development process.

## Policy advisory

The Institute participates in United Nations AI and data-governance work at the “By Regulation” end of its four-phase strategy — the point where principle-level commitments are translated into public, national-scale frameworks. Its UN CSTD committee role has a public record.

## MLSecOps and OWASP

ML security work studies how the standard software-security taxonomy fails to cover machine-learning-specific risk — model artifacts, training pipelines, and inference endpoints all introduce failure modes the OWASP Top 10 was not written for. The MLSecOps Top 10 is the Institute's applied answer: ten vulnerability classes mapped from OWASP categories onto the ML lifecycle, from unrestricted model endpoints to supply-chain risk in ML code.

## Principles and frameworks

Technical assurance and evaluation asks whether an AI system is fit for a given deployment — not only whether it is accurate, but whether its infrastructure, processes and monitoring are mature enough to support the claim. This work underlies the AI-RFX Procurement Framework and the ML Maturity Model: both translate assurance questions into criteria an organisation can evaluate a supplier or system against.
